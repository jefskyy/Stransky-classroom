# Thermodynamic Variables Summary Table

| Variable Name | Symbol | Description (1 sentence max) | Common SI Units |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Heat Transfer | $Q$ | Total amount of thermal energy transferred across a system boundary due to a temperature difference. | $[\text{J}]$ or $[\text{kJ}]$ |
| Heat Transfer Rate | $\dot{Q}$ | Thermal energy transferred per unit time across a system boundary. | $[\text{W}]$ or $[\text{kW}]$ |
| Specific Heat Transfer | $q$ | Heat transfer per unit mass of the system. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}]$ |
| Work | $W$ | Total energy transferred by a force acting through a distance across a system boundary. | $[\text{J}]$ or $[\text{kJ}]$ |
| Work Rate (Power) | $\dot{W}$ | Work done or energy transferred per unit time. | $[\text{W}]$ or $[\text{kW}]$ |
| Specific Work | $w$ | Work done per unit mass of the system. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}]$ |
| Thermal / Cycle Efficiency | $\eta$ or $\eta_{\text{th}}$ | Ratio of net useful work output to total thermal energy input for a cycle. | $[-]$ or $[\%]$ |
| Temperature | $T$ | Measure of the average macroscopic kinetic energy of the particles within a substance. | $[\text{K}]$ or $[^\circ\text{C}]$ |
| Pressure | $P$ | Normal force exerted by a fluid per unit surface area of its boundary. | $[\text{Pa}]$ or $[\text{kPa}]$ |
| Internal Energy | $U$ | Total microscopic kinetic and potential energy contained within a system. | $[\text{J}]$ or $[\text{kJ}]$ |
| Specific Internal Energy | $u$ | Internal energy contained per unit mass of the system. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}]$ |
| Enthalpy | $H$ | Total thermodynamic property defined as the sum of internal energy and the product of pressure and volume. | $[\text{J}]$ or $[\text{kJ}]$ |
| Specific Enthalpy | $h$ | Enthalpy per unit mass of the system. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}]$ |
| Entropy | $S$ | Measure of the microscopic molecular disorder or randomness within a system. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{K}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{K}}]$ |
| Specific Entropy | $s$ | Entropy per unit mass of the system. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}]$ |
| Constant-Volume Specific Heat | $c_v$ | Amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of a substance by one degree at constant volume. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}]$ |
| Constant-Pressure Specific Heat | $c_p$ | Amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of a substance by one degree at constant pressure. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}]$ |
| Kinetic Energy | $\text{KE}$ | Energy possessed by a system due to its overall macroscopic motion. | $[\text{J}]$ or $[\text{kJ}]$ |
| Potential Energy | $\text{PE}$ | Energy possessed by a system due to its position within a gravitational field. | $[\text{J}]$ or $[\text{kJ}]$ |
| Mass | $m$ | Measure of the total amount of matter contained within a system. | $[\text{kg}]$ |
| Mass Flow Rate | $\dot{m}$ | Mass of fluid passing through a cross-section per unit time. | $[\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{s}}]$ |
| Density | $\rho$ | Mass contained per unit volume of a substance. | $[\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}]$ |
| Volume | $V$ | Total three-dimensional spatial extent occupied by a system. | $[\text{m}^3]$ |
| Specific Volume | $v$ | Volume occupied per unit mass of a substance, representing the reciprocal of density. | $[\frac{\text{m}^3}{\text{kg}}]$ |
| Volumetric Flow Rate | $\dot{V}$ | Volume of fluid passing through a cross-section per unit time. | $[\frac{\text{m}^3}{\text{s}}]$ |
| Velocity | $V$ or $V_{\text{flow}}$ | Magnitude of macroscopic fluid displacement per unit time. | $[\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}]$ |
| Specific Gas Constant | $R$ | Substance-specific gas constant calculated as the universal gas constant divided by molar mass. | $[\frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}]$ or $[\frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}]$ |
